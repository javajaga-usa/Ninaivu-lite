    1. Before you start

    Ninaivu Lite shows your family's photos and videos to everyone at home, on any phone, tablet or computer on the same Wi-Fi. It runs on one computer in the house. It only reads your photo folders: it never moves, edits or deletes a photograph, and nothing leaves the house. (The importer copies photos into the library from old drives, and Sudar can save an edited copy beside an original; neither changes a photograph you have.)

    What you need

    • A computer that stays on at home: Windows 10 or 11, a Mac, a Linux PC, or a Raspberry Pi 4 or 5 (64-bit).
    • Your photos in one or more folders on that computer, or on a drive plugged into it.
    • Phones, tablets or computers on the same Wi-Fi, with a current browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox, or Safari 16.4 or newer).

    Install it

    The simplest way is a download from the Releases page of github.com/javajaga-usa/Ninaivu-lite. Each one carries everything it needs, including its own Python.

Then use the portable zip, or start Ninaivu Lite from a copy of the source: install Python 3.10 or newer from python.org (tick Add python.exe to PATH), unzip the download to a permanent folder such as C:\Ninaivu-lite, and double-click start.cmd (./start.sh on a Mac or Linux). The first start takes a minute or two and needs the internet once; keep that window open while the family uses it.

    The Control Panel

    The Control Panel is a small window on the computer that runs Ninaivu Lite. It opens after installation, and at any time from the Ninaivu Lite Control Panel shortcut (in the portable zip, Ninaivu Lite.exe; in a copy of the source, Start - Ninaivu Lite Control Panel.vbs or ./start.sh --panel).

    1. Press Start. The status changes to Running.
    2. Read the addresses under Where to open it. Use the On your phone address (such as http://192.168.1.20:8080/) on phones and tablets.
    3. Until the administrator is made, the panel also shows the Setup code. It is asked for only when the administrator is made from another device (see chapter 2).
    4. Press Open the family app for the gallery, or Open the console for the administrator's pages.
    5. Tick Start Ninaivu Lite when I sign in once, so it starts with the computer.
    6. Closing the panel leaves Ninaivu Lite running. Stop stops it; Open the log and Open the data folder help when something needs looking into.
    7. Ninaivu Lite never looks for updates by itself. Before running a newer setup file, press Stop and close the panel. Photos, people, settings and the index are kept.
    Servers: on a Raspberry Pi or a server without a screen, the Linux installer sets up a service that starts Ninaivu Lite at boot. Installed with sudo, there is no Control Panel: use sudo systemctl start|stop|restart ninaivu-lite.

    2. The first visit: make the administrator

    The administrator decides what everyone else can see. Do this once, on the computer itself if you can.

    1. Open http://localhost:8080 (or press Open the family app in the Control Panel).
    2. Choose the language at the bottom: English or தமிழ்.
    3. Fill in Your name, a Username (lowercase, no spaces) and a Password of at least 8 characters.
    4. Press Create profile.
    5. If you do this from a phone instead of the computer, it also asks for the short Setup code. The Control Panel shows it (Figure 1). It is also written to logs/ninaivu-lite.log in the data folder (Open the log), and printed in the window when Ninaivu Lite was started from a copy of the source. On a Linux service, run journalctl -u ninaivu-lite | grep code (with --user if it was installed without sudo); with Docker, docker logs ninaivu-lite.

    Press Open the admin console. The console opens with the first-day walk-through.

    3. The first-day walk-through

    Four short steps: the library folder, old photos to bring in, the household, and what the family can do. Skip for now leaves any of them for later.

    Step 1: where are the photographs?

    1. Press Choose a folder.
    2. Click through the folders, or type the path in the box at the bottom (for example D:\Photos).
    3. Press Use this folder. Indexing starts at once; photos appear as they are found.
    4. Press Next.

    Step 2: bring in old photos

    1. Type the top folder of an old drive or card and press Add folder, or use Browse…. Every folder inside it is scanned, however deep.
    2. Check Where the archive is built. By default it is inside your library folder, so the family sees the photos as they arrive.
    3. Press Start the import. Every file is copied and checked; the originals are never changed, moved or deleted.
    4. Press Next. The import carries on in the background; you can watch it on the Import page. If there is nothing to bring in, skip this step: Import, under Library, does the same at any time.

    Step 3: who is in the household?

    1. Type a name, choose Family member or Guest, and type a PIN of 4 to 8 digits (or leave it empty). A guest gets a password instead.
    2. Press Add. The person appears in the list above. Repeat for everyone.
    3. A PIN that is too easy to guess, such as 1234, is refused with a message under the form. Choose another.
    4. Press Next.
    Tip: A family member without a PIN can be opened from any phone or computer on your home network. Give a PIN to anyone whose photographs should stay theirs.

    Step 4: what your family can do

    Read it, then press Open the console.

    4. The admin console

    The console is at http://<computer>:8080/admin. Only administrators can open it; it asks for the username and password.

    Overview

    Under What each role sees, choose a role or a person to see the library exactly as they would. The library card at the bottom lists the folders and the address for the family app.

    Library settings

    • Add a folder adds another photo folder (another drive, for example).
    • Watch the folder and index new files automatically picks up new photos by itself as they are copied in.
    • Rescan all looks for changes now; Full re-index reads everything again from the start.
    • Remove takes a folder out of the library, after asking. The photos on disk are not touched, and a copy of the index is taken just before. Who sees what, favourites, albums and share links come back if the same folder is added again.
    • Moved? (The photographs are somewhere else now: choose where) appears beside a folder that cannot be found. If its photos are somewhere else now (a new drive letter, a new computer), press it and choose the new place: the folder points there and keeps everything.
    Tip: The first scan makes a small preview of every photo. A computer with several cores makes them on all of its cores but one (three at a time on a Raspberry Pi, seven on an eight-core laptop) and always leaves one core for the gallery, so the family can look at photos while it works. Later visits to the gallery open at once until something in the library changes.

    Import: old drives into one archive

    1. Sources: add each old drive, card or backup folder with Add source. Every folder inside is scanned.
    2. Destination: where the archive is built, filed as YYYY/MM/DD by the day each photo was taken. Choose the archive's own root; pick a folder inside one and Ninaivu uses the root instead, and says so.
    3. Run: Start consolidation copies and checks every file (hashed as it is read, read back from the archive before it counts). Duplicates are found by content and left where they are; the same name with different bytes gets _1. Start also resumes an interrupted run. Dry run decides everything and writes nothing; Audit archive re-reads every archived file and re-checks its hash. Imported photos appear in the gallery while the import runs.
    4. When it finishes, Add to library makes the archive part of the gallery (an archive inside the library folder, as here, already is). A Google Photos export (Takeout) comes across with its dates, places and descriptions, and Make the albums recreates its albums once the archive is indexed. Export manifest writes a list of every file with its hash.
    Not finished? If the drive is unplugged or the destination fills up part-way, the run ends as not finished and says so. Plug the drive back in (or make room) and press Start consolidation to finish; what was copied is kept.

    People

    1. To add someone, press Add someone.
    2. Fill in the name and username, choose the role and the library folder they may see, and how they sign in: Tap to enter — no secret, a PIN, or a password.
    3. Press Create profile.
    4. For anyone already listed you can change the role, folder or entry, Disable them, Reset password, Sign out everywhere, or Delete them.
    RoleCan
    AdminEverything: folders, people, who sees what, settings.
    Family memberSee Public and Family photos, download, favourites, albums, share links.
    GuestSee Public photos only.

    You can also give a person one folder (for example only Trips): they then see nothing outside it.

    Visibility: who sees what

    Every photo is Everyone (public: guests and Just looking visitors too), Family (the default) or Nobody (hidden: administrators only). Set it per folder here; the rule covers everything inside the folder, including photos added later.

    1. Find the folder (press the arrow to open the folders inside it).
    2. Press Everyone, Family or Nobody on its row.
    3. If this would show photos that are now more private, a box asks first. Press Show them anyway to go ahead.
    4. A banner shows the last change with Undo that change, in case you change your mind.
    Tip: Administrators can also change one photo while viewing it, with the three buttons in the viewer's bar.

    Settings

    • This home: the name shown at the top of the family app, and the default language for new visitors.
    • Let visitors browse public media without signing in: the Just looking tile. Untick it if the library should be private.
    • Send videos to guests and share links as they are when their location cannot be removed: off by default. A video is sent to a guest or a share link with its location removed, which needs ffmpeg on the computer; without ffmpeg such a video is not shared unless this is ticked (it then keeps its location). See chapter 8.
    • Backups: Download a backup gives you a full recovery zip to keep somewhere else: the index with people, albums, favourites, share links and who sees what, the settings, and profile pictures. Keep it private: it holds everyone's password and PIN checks and the share links. Ninaivu Lite also makes one by itself every day; Settings lists the copies kept, and says so if the last daily copy failed (chapter 12).
    • Moving up to Ninaivu: Export for Ninaivu is an optional file, if you ever move the library to the full Ninaivu.

    5. Plugging in a pendrive, a drive or a phone

    Plug a USB pendrive, a memory card, an external hard drive or a phone (by its cable) into the computer Ninaivu Lite runs on. The console, and the Control Panel if it is open, ask what to do with it.

    • Import media from this drive opens the Import page with the drive as the source. Check the destination and press Start consolidation. Nothing on the drive is changed.
    • Export media to this drive copies the library's photos and videos onto the drive, into a Ninaivu Lite folder, keeping the library's own folders. Next time only what is new is copied; nothing already on the drive is overwritten or deleted. When it finishes, eject the drive before unplugging it.
    • Not now asks again only when the drive is next plugged in.

    A phone is offered Import media from this phone only. Unlock it and choose File transfer (Android) or Trust this computer (iPhone), or Windows cannot see its photos. On Windows the camera folders (DCIM, Pictures, Movies) are copied across the cable, imported into the archive, and the temporary copies removed; next time only new photos come across. Nothing on the phone is changed. On Linux, open the phone in the file manager first; on a Mac, phones are not detected.

    Tip: The computer's own disks, and a drive the library itself is on, are never asked about. When Ninaivu Lite was installed on Linux with sudo, it runs as its own account and may not see drives or phones your desktop opens: add their folders under Import yourself.

    6. The family gallery on a computer

    • Left menu: All media, Favourites, Photos, Videos; for administrators also Hidden and Public. Below them your albums, a row of years, and the folders with their counts.
    • Top bar: search, the view menu, the thumbnail-size slider, the language button (அ) and your profile.
    • Date bar: on the right edge, drag to jump to a month or year.

    Search and folders

    1. Type in the search box: a file name, a folder, a year or month, or a camera name. Suggestions appear as you type; press Enter.
    2. Or click a folder or a year in the left menu.
    3. A chip at the top shows what is filtered. Press × on it, or Clear all, to see everything again.

    Selecting photos and making an album

    1. Press Select all beside a day, or tick photos one by one.
    2. The bar at the top offers Favourite, Download (as a zip), Add to album and, for administrators, who may see them.
    3. Press Add to album, type a new album name and press Create, or pick an existing album.
    4. The album appears under Albums in the left menu. Open it to rename, share or delete it.

    Favourites

    Each person keeps their own favourites. Press ♡ in the viewer (or Favourite on a selection), and find them under Favourites in the left menu.

    8. Sharing with someone outside the family

    1. In the viewer press the share button (Share link), or Share album on an open album.
    2. Choose when it expires: 7 days, 30 days or never. Add a password or PIN if you like; whoever has the link can try it 60 times a day at most.
    3. Press Generate Link, then Copy, and send the link to the person.
    4. Your list of links is in your profile, where any link can be ended at any time.

    Hidden photographs. A Hidden photograph cannot be shared: make it Family or Public first. A link to a photo stops working if the photo is later set to Hidden.

    Videos. A video is sent with its location removed too, which needs ffmpeg on the computer. Without it, a guest or someone with a link is told the video cannot be shared, unless an administrator ticks Send videos to guests and share links as they are when their location cannot be removed in Settings; the video then keeps its location.

    Tip: The visitor gets a copy without location or camera details, and does not see the file name. Links work only on your home network: Ninaivu Lite is not meant to be reached from the internet.

    9. Sudar, the photo studio

    Sudar edits a photo in the browser. Nothing is sent anywhere and the original is never changed. Open it from the viewer: the wand button (Edit with Sudar (AI)), or on a phone ⋮ → Edit with Sudar (AI).

    1. In Adjust, pick a look (Vivid, Golden hour, Cinematic and others), a suggestion, or move the sliders for light, colour and detail.
    2. Drag the slider across the photo to compare the original and the edit.
    3. In AI assist, describe the change in plain words, such as make it warmer, and press Plan edits. Review the plan before applying it.
    4. When it looks right, press Download image, or as an administrator Save copy to Ninaivu library to keep a copy beside the original.

    10. Your profile and signing out

    • Press your initials at the top right. Your profile lets you change your name, colour and password, and lists your share links.
    • Your picture: open a Family or Public photo, then choose Use as my profile picture. Remove picture in your profile puts the initials back; an administrator can remove anyone's from People. The picture is shown to whoever reaches the sign-in screen, so a Hidden photo cannot be used; choose one you are happy to show.
    • Sign out (the door symbol) returns to the sign-in screen.

    11. On a phone or tablet

    Open the On your phone address from the Control Panel, such as http://192.168.1.20:8080/, in the phone's browser. Add it to the home screen to open it like an app. The phone keeps Ninaivu Lite's own files until it is updated, so the gallery opens quickly on later visits; after an update it fetches the new ones by itself. Held sideways, the sign-in screens and dialogs scroll, so every button can be reached.

    1. Tap your own picture. A profile with no secret opens straight away.
    2. If it asks for a PIN or password, type it and tap Enter. ← Someone else goes back to the pictures.
    3. On a tablet the family shares, signing in as someone else signs the previous person out of it, so nobody stays signed in as them.
    4. Just looking shows only public photos, with no sign-in.
    5. On a phone a row of years sits above the photos: tap one to jump to it. Tap ☰ for the menu with albums and folders.

    In the viewer, swipe left or right to move and down to close. The everyday buttons stay on the bar; the rest (Edit with Sudar (AI), slideshow, ambient frame, profile picture, add to album) are behind ⋮. To select several photos, press and hold one, then tap others.

    12. Looking after it

    • Backups: every day Ninaivu Lite makes a full recovery zip (the index with people, albums, favourites, share links and visibility, the settings, and profile pictures) in backups/ in its data folder, and keeps the last 7 days, plus one a week for 4 weeks and one a month for 3 months. It also takes one just before a library folder or a person is removed. Settings lists the copies kept, and says so if the last daily copy failed. Settings → Download a backup gives you one to keep elsewhere; keep it private, as it holds everyone's password and PIN checks and the share links. To put one back, stop Ninaivu Lite and run the command below for how it was installed, with --restore <zip> in place of --reset-password <username>; what was there is kept in a before-restore-… folder inside the data folder, never deleted. Everyone signs in again afterwards. If the restored folders are not where they were (a new computer), use Moved? on Library settings. Your photos are not in the zip: they are your own files, so back them up as you always do.
    • Data folder: %LOCALAPPDATA%\Ninaivu-lite on Windows, ~/Library/Application Support/Ninaivu-lite on a Mac, ~/.local/share/ninaivu-lite on Linux; the data folder beside the program for the portable zip; /var/lib/ninaivu-lite for Linux installed with sudo; the ninaivu_lite_data volume for Docker. It holds settings, the index, previews, backups and logs, never your photos.
    • Who can reach it: Ninaivu Lite answers phones and computers on your home network, a private VPN such as Tailscale, and the computer it runs on. A request from the internet (mobile data through a port forwarded on the router, or a tunnel such as ngrok or Cloudflare Tunnel) is refused with Ninaivu Lite answers only the home network. Only someone who puts HTTPS of their own in front of it should change this: stop Ninaivu Lite and set "allow_internet": true in settings.json in the data folder (or the environment variable NINAIVU_ALLOW_INTERNET=1; with Docker, ALLOW_INTERNET=1).
    • Forgot the admin password? Stop Ninaivu Lite, then on that computer run the command for how it was installed:
      • Windows installer, or Linux without sudo: ninaivu-lite --reset-password <username> (on Windows, in a new Command Prompt).
      • Linux with sudo: sudo systemctl stop ninaivu-lite, then sudo -u ninaivu-lite /usr/local/bin/ninaivu-lite --reset-password <username>, then sudo systemctl start ninaivu-lite.
      • Portable zip: in its Ninaivu Lite folder, app\Python\python.exe -m ninaivu_lite --data "<that folder>\data" --reset-password <username>.
      • A copy of the source: python -m ninaivu_lite --reset-password <username> (inside the folder, after .venv\Scripts\activate on Windows or source .venv/bin/activate elsewhere).
      • Docker: docker compose -f installers/docker/docker-compose.yml run --rm ninaivu-lite python -m ninaivu_lite --data /data --reset-password <username>.
    • A drive unplugged? Its photos show as unavailable and come back when it returns. Nothing is lost.
    • Updating: Ninaivu Lite never checks for a newer version and asks nothing of GitHub; download one from the releases page when you choose to. First stop Ninaivu Lite: in the Control Panel press Stop and close it; on a Linux server, sudo systemctl stop ninaivu-lite. If you forget, the Windows and Linux installers offer to stop it and wait. Then, with an installer, run the new installer. On Linux, run the newer .sh the same way as the first. On a Mac, drag the new app over the old one in Applications; if Ninaivu Lite was still running, the Control Panel says so: press Restart. For the portable zip, press Stop and untick Start Ninaivu Lite when I sign in in the old copy's Control Panel, extract the new version to a new folder, copy the old data folder into it, and tick the box in the new copy's Control Panel. For a copy of the source, replace the program files and start it again. Your settings, people, index, backups and photos are untouched. The first start after an update brings the index forward by itself, after keeping a copy of it as it was (backups/before-update-…zip). Going back from 1.10.0 to 1.9.0, 1.8.0 or 1.7.0 needs nothing special. Going back to 1.6.0 or older means restoring a backup made before the update (see Backups above): version 1.6.0 cannot open the index of 1.7.0 or later.
    • Linux, installed with sudo: Ninaivu Lite runs as a service under its own ninaivu-lite account, never as root, with its data in /var/lib/ninaivu-lite, and there is no Control Panel: use sudo systemctl start|stop|restart ninaivu-lite. That account must be able to read your photo folders and pass through every folder above them. The installer names any it cannot read; let it in with, for example:
      sudo setfacl -R -m u:ninaivu-lite:rX /home/me/Pictures (what is there now)
      sudo setfacl -R -d -m u:ninaivu-lite:rX /home/me/Pictures (what is added later)
      sudo setfacl -m u:ninaivu-lite:x /home/me (each folder above it)
      sudo systemctl restart ninaivu-lite
      It may not see drives your desktop opens; add their folders under Import yourself.
    • Moving up to Ninaivu (optional): for faces, search by description or cloud backup, the full Ninaivu can take over your library. Use Settings → Export for Ninaivu, or run the command above with --export lite-export.json, and give that file to Ninaivu.

    13. Common questions

    QuestionAnswer
    The phone can't open the address.Use the address shown in the Control Panel (not localhost), make sure the phone is on the same Wi-Fi, and allow Ninaivu Lite (Python) through the Windows firewall when asked. On Linux, let port 8080 through the firewall: sudo ufw allow 8080/tcp (Ubuntu, Raspberry Pi OS) or sudo firewall-cmd --permanent --add-port=8080/tcp && sudo firewall-cmd --reload (Fedora).
    "This address is not one Ninaivu Lite answers to."Ninaivu Lite answers only to this computer's own names: its addresses (such as http://192.168.1.20:8080), its network name (mypc or mypc.local) and localhost. To use another name (a name of your own, or a reverse proxy), stop Ninaivu Lite and add it to "allowed_hosts" in settings.json in the data folder, for example "allowed_hosts": ["photos.home"], or to the NINAIVU_ALLOWED_HOSTS environment variable (names separated by commas; with Docker, ALLOWED_HOSTS), then start it again.
    "Ninaivu Lite answers only the home network."The phone or computer reached it from the internet, not from home: on mobile data through a port forwarded on the router, or through a tunnel. Connect it to the home Wi-Fi, or to a private VPN such as Tailscale, and use the address from the Control Panel. To allow the internet on purpose, see Who can reach it in chapter 12.
    A guest or a share link says a video cannot be shared.Install ffmpeg on the computer: Ninaivu Lite needs it to remove a video's location before sending it. Or, as the administrator, tick Send videos to guests and share links as they are when their location cannot be removed in Settings (the video then keeps its location).
    Some videos show a plain tile.A video gets its preview picture the first time a family member or administrator scrolls past it or plays it. A video the browser cannot play keeps the plain tile (install ffmpeg on the computer for those); it can still be downloaded.
    iPhone HEIC photos show a plain tile.Install the optional HEIC support (pip install pillow-heif in Ninaivu Lite's Python: .venv\Scripts\pip on Windows, .venv/bin/pip elsewhere), then restart.
    Port 8080 is busy.Ninaivu Lite uses the next free port and shows it in the Control Panel; or choose one with --port.
    Someone forgot their PIN.An administrator opens People in the console and uses Reset password, or changes how that person signs in.
    Can people outside the house see the photos?No. Ninaivu Lite answers only the home network and private VPNs, and refuses requests from the internet. Do not open it to the internet: it uses plain HTTP.
